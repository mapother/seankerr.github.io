Does AI actually work?

Artificial intelligence is consuming extraordinary amounts of money, infrastructure and organizational attention.

I am interested in a simpler question: Is it actually delivering?

I investigate whether AI works in the real world — economically, operationally and institutionally.

Three questions

01

Does it pay?

AI economics

What is AI actually costing organizations? Where is it producing measurable economic value? Where is it not? What happens when infrastructure, integration, inference and organizational costs are included in the calculation?

02

Can we depend on it?

AI dependency

AI is becoming operational infrastructure. What happens when mission-critical capability depends on cloud connectivity, communications, APIs, external services and supporting infrastructure that may not always be available?

03

Are we ready for it?

AI consequences

How do institutions understand, govern and oversee AI as adoption accelerates? Where are the gaps between policy and practice, and how do we create accountability without preventing useful innovation?

Does it pay?

Independent research & advisory

Organizations are spending heavily on models, infrastructure, consultants, vendors and integration. Yet the relationship between AI expenditure and economic return remains surprisingly difficult to see.

I am particularly interested in talking with executives and practitioners willing to discuss what is actually happening inside their organizations: What did you expect AI to do? What did it cost? What happened?

This work informs my writing and a limited amount of independent advisory work with organizations confronting difficult AI investment, economics and deployment questions.

Why these questions?

My route into artificial intelligence has not been conventional.

My professional life has crossed capital markets, defense intelligence, enterprise technology, project and product delivery, software and data, entrepreneurship and public-interest research.

I have worked in environments where complicated systems, risk, infrastructure and institutional decision-making have real consequences. Along the way I continued building technically through project and agile disciplines, data science, Python, decentralized finance and artificial intelligence.

That history has left me less interested in AI as spectacle than in what happens after the demonstration.

Does it create value? Can we depend on it? What happens when institutions begin depending upon systems they do not completely understand?

Those are the questions I am pursuing now.
